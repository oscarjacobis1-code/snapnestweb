CHOOSING A PROVIDER · GUYANA

How do you choose a website developer in Guyana?

Do not start with who has the fanciest homepage. Start with whether they can prove they understand the job you actually need done.

Guyana has agencies, freelancers and software companies that can build websites. The useful question is not “who is the best?” It is “who is the best fit for this scope, budget and level of operational responsibility?”

1. Look at live work, not mockups

Ask for public projects you can open yourself. Check the site on your phone. Try the navigation and forms. A portfolio should show that the provider has shipped work, not only designed screenshots.

2. Ask what happens after launch

Who handles hosting, SSL, backups, security updates and small changes? A low build price can become expensive if nobody owns the operational side after the site goes live.

3. Confirm who owns the domain and accounts

The business should know where its domain is registered and who controls the account. The same applies to analytics, hosting, email and third-party services. Avoid arrangements where leaving the developer means losing access to your own digital assets.

4. Separate a website from a business system

If all you need is a brochure site, do not pay for a complex application. But if you need appointments, orders, inventory, staff access or customer records, do not let someone price that work as if it were “just another page.”

5. Ask how they handle search visibility

At minimum, the site should have sensible page titles and descriptions, canonical URLs, a sitemap, crawlable links and Google Search Console setup. SEO is not a magic switch, but a site should not launch invisible because the basics were skipped.

6. Demand a written scope

The scope should tell you what is being built, what is not included, the implementation price, recurring costs, payment milestones and what support looks like. If those details are vague before payment, they usually stay vague after payment.
