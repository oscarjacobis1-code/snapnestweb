WEBSITE COSTS · GUYANA

How much does a business website cost in Guyana?

There is no useful single answer because “a website” can mean a four-page business presence, a booking system, an online store or a custom operating platform. The right comparison starts with scope.

The price difference in Guyana is not mainly about how many colours or animations a website has. It is about what the site must do, how much content must be created, what it connects to, and who is responsible after launch.

Current public market examples

Published Guyana pricing shows a very wide market. Firelinkx currently publishes website ranges from US$399–US$999 for starter sites and US$999–US$1,800 for small-business sites, with more involved booking, e-commerce and advanced work rising into the thousands of US dollars. NextGen Digital Guyana publishes GYD 200,000–450,000 for one of its upper website packages and GYD 250,000–450,000 for a small e-commerce store.

SnapNest's current public pricing uses a different structure: a Foundation system starts from GYD 55,000 implementation plus GYD 15,000 per month for hosting, maintenance and support. The important word is starts. Booking, ordering, staff workflows, inventory, integrations and custom requirements change the scope.

What actually changes the price?

  • Content: whether the business supplies usable text, prices and photos or the provider has to create them.
  • Customer actions: simple enquiries cost less than booking, ordering, payment or account features.
  • Operations: connecting the website to stock, staff, customer records or reporting turns a website into a business system.
  • Design detail: a custom branded experience takes more work than a standard layout.
  • Integrations: payment, email, CRM, maps, accounting and other external services add testing and ongoing dependencies.
  • Support: hosting, backups, maintenance and changes after launch are real operating costs, not an afterthought.

Do not compare only the build price

A GYD 60,000 quote and a GYD 300,000 quote may not be competing for the same job. Ask both providers what happens after launch, who owns the domain, whether hosting is included, whether changes cost extra, what happens if something breaks, and whether SEO basics such as titles, descriptions, sitemap and indexing are included.

What should a small business buy first?

If the problem is credibility and discovery, a focused website may be enough. If the business already loses appointments, orders, customer details or stock information, fixing only the public website may leave the expensive problem untouched. That is why SnapNest starts with the workflow rather than assuming every business needs the same package.
